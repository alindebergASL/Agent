# University of Iowa: Responsible AI meeting brief

*November 23, 2026 · Discussion with university staff*

**Meeting goal:** Pick one low-risk task to pilot, decide who will verify the output, and identify whom to consult before using sensitive data.

## What Iowa’s guidance says

- **Use an approved tool and check the data first.** Iowa’s responsible-use page requires a university-approved AI tool for university work. Check the data classification and the tool’s restrictions before entering information. If a service has no university agreement, do not share nonpublic institutional data with it. Student records and other sensitive information may require additional review. [Using AI Responsibly](https://ai.uiowa.edu/using-ai-responsibly)
- **Keep a person accountable.** Users are responsible for the AI output they rely on: verify information and apply human judgment. Implementing a new AI tool is subject to security review. [Guidance and Policies](https://ai.uiowa.edu/guidance-and-policies)

## Practical resources

- **Staff:** Iowa’s staff guide gives examples by risk level. Drafting routine emails is low risk when staff avoid sensitive information and check accuracy. A subject-matter expert should review AI-drafted policies or procedures before adoption. [AI for Administration and Staff](https://ai.uiowa.edu/ai-administration-and-staff)
- **Faculty and instructors:** The teaching guide offers sample syllabus language for setting AI expectations and advises against using AI detectors on student work. [Artificial Intelligence Tools and Teaching](https://teach.its.uiowa.edu/artificial-intelligence-tools-and-teaching) The Center for Teaching’s [AI Assessment Scale](https://teaching.center.uiowa.edu/ai-assessment-scale-reflecting-ai-assignment-design) helps instructors specify what AI use is allowed on each assignment.
- **Students:** There is no single campus-wide rule on when AI is allowed in coursework. Check the syllabus and assignment instructions, ask the instructor if unsure, and acknowledge AI use when required. Unauthorized use may be academic misconduct. [AI Guidance for Students](https://ai.uiowa.edu/ai-guidance-students) The [student resource page](https://ai.uiowa.edu/students) points to Copilot Chat and campus workshops.
- **Tools and training:** Copilot Chat is available to faculty, staff, and students at no additional fee. Paid faculty and staff options include Microsoft 365 Copilot, ChatGPT Edu, and Claude for Education; check each tool’s data restrictions. [University-Supported AI Tools](https://ai.uiowa.edu/university-supported-ai-tools) ITS lists instructor-led training and on-demand resources, and its Emerging Technologies team can advise on tool choice and data handling. [ITS Generative AI Training and Resources](https://its.uiowa.edu/generative-ai-training-and-resources)

## Two questions to ask

1. If we want to summarize student feedback that includes names or IDs, what must we remove, which university tool and account may we use, and who should confirm that before we upload anything? [Using AI Responsibly](https://ai.uiowa.edu/using-ai-responsibly)
2. Could we pilot AI-assisted drafting for public event announcements, and who would verify dates, locations, and registration details before publication? [Using AI Responsibly](https://ai.uiowa.edu/using-ai-responsibly)
